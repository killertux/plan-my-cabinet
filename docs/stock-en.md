# Project stock

For sending a cutting packet and managing outdated revisions, see [Shop handoff](shop-handoff-en.md).

Use **Add sheet or offcut** to declare a rectangular physical stock piece. Choose a material, enter its measured length, width and thickness (independent of the material's default), and choose stock grain along length (X), width (Y), nondirectional or unknown. Mark it **Owned** or **To purchase**. A blank price means unknown, while `0` is a known zero price; prices use the project's currency and are per piece.

Quantity 1–10,000 creates separately identified pieces in one undoable action. Identical offcuts are still separate pieces. The list shows a short ID for each. Use **Edit piece** to correct an individual piece, or **Move up/down** to set its visible priority for later first-fit allocation. Edits to dimensions, grain or trims do not silently move existing allocations. Stock is stored in this project only; declaring it never reserves it in another project.

## Automatic first-fit and allocation status

Creating or duplicating a board tries the declared physical stock pieces in the visible **Move up/down** priority order (owned and to-purchase pieces alike). For each compatible piece it tries unrotated, then permitted quarter-turn orientations, and deterministic sheet origins; only a position with a verified full-span cut sequence is used. Material identity, effective thickness, usable dimensions, grain and kerf must agree. Existing allocations stay exactly where they are, including unlocked ones; neither assembly poses nor other sheet positions are repacked. This is a bounded first fit, not an optimization or proof that no layout exists. It never creates extra purchase pieces. If no fit is found or the search limit is reached, the new board remains editable and **Unallocated**; declare more stock, change an explicit restriction or place it yourself.

The allocation overview counts every board once, including hidden assembly boards, and lists unallocated, conflicted or search-budget issues; valid boards appear in the total but not the issue rows. Use **Select and reveal** on an issue to locate the part in the linked views and show a hidden part without changing its stock demand. Red/crossed sheet shapes retain their recorded positions after a board resize or a material, grain, stock, kerf or trim change. Conflict reasons include material/thickness/grain mismatch, outside usable bounds, overlap, inadequate kerf clearance and unsupported full-span cut sequence. A search-budget result means **feasibility not established**, not a proven impossible layout or a valid cut witness. Resolve issues before treating a layout as ready for cutting; an unallocated board remains a draft issue even if the other sheets are verified.

## Edit sheet / Repair session

Select a part in the 2D sheet, unallocated list, or 3D view, then choose **Edit sheet / Repair session**. Drag an unlocked allocated part on its current sheet: the outline follows the pointer with feasibility feedback, and releasing stages the new position. Alternatively enter sheet X and Y origins and choose **Stage placement / transfer**; select another declared sheet to transfer it, or toggle **Quarter-turn** where grain permits it. Numeric entry also allocates an unallocated board. Length inputs accept mm, cm, m, in, ft and inch fractions. Values needing rounding to 0.001 mm show the proposed value and require explicit consent. **Lock placement** prevents repositioning, rotation or transfer until **Unlock placement**; **Unallocate** explicitly removes even a locked part. A lock does not prevent a design resize from invalidating its placement.

Several changes can be staged together while intermediate conflicts remain visible in red with reasons. For example, after a resize overlaps two parts, unlock a locked placement, stage one move, inspect the remaining conflict, then stage a second move or **Unallocate** before accepting. **Accept repair** commits one undoable change only when every affected sheet has a verified full-span cutting sequence; a rejected acceptance leaves the staged preview available for further repair. A search-limit result is unknown, not a valid witness. Unallocated parts remain drafts; unrelated old conflicts do not block explicit removal. **Cancel repair** or Escape discards the entire preview. Sheet placement never changes the part's assembly pose; both views share the part's identity.

To reproduce the headless editor walkthrough: declare a 220 × 50 × 18 mm sheet with 5 mm kerf and matching lengthwise grain, create two 100 × 50 × 18 mm boards and confirm first-fit at X = 0 and 105 mm. Group them into an assembly and resize the first board to 115 mm using its **End** anchor. Its assembly end face stays fixed while the original sheet origin is retained and overlaps the second board. In a repair session, unlock if needed and move the second allocation to X = 120 mm; verify the 115 + 5 + 100 mm cut sequence, accept, save a `.pmcab` file and reopen through the project API. IDs, assembly poses, allocations and the reconstructed cut witness should agree with the saved state. This is an API walkthrough; the desktop GUI save-transfer-reopen walkthrough follows when file controls are integrated in task 11.2.

Trim values are exact nonnegative millimetres (left, right, bottom, top); zero needs no trim. Their opposite-edge sums must leave positive usable length and width. Trim represents total edge loss, including the blade kerf when cutting plans are added. All length fields accept mm, cm, m, in and ft (suffixes can override the project's display unit); precision requiring rounding needs explicit confirmation. The dialog supports Tab navigation and Escape/Cancel; invalid or cancelled input leaves the project unchanged.

## Cutting assumptions and worked examples

The cutting model uses a positive **kerf** (blade width) for each physical split. Every cut is straight, parallel to an edge, and runs all the way across the **current rectangular piece** (which may be an intermediate offcut, not the original sheet). The full kerf band must remain inside that piece: along the cut axis, input = first output + kerf + second output. Each finished part must be an exact rectangular output; unused rectangular outputs are offcuts or waste. One split is **one physical cut**, including an edge-shaving or trim pass. A shared split is not charged once per part. A boundary already supplied by the stock needs no cut. Stacking pieces, stopped cuts, interior cutouts, angled cuts and blades extending beyond the stock edge are outside this model.

- With a 5 mm kerf, a 205 × 100 mm piece splits into two 100 × 100 mm parts in **one cut**: 100 + 5 + 100 = 205 mm. The second part begins 105 mm from the left edge. A 204 × 100 mm piece leaves only 99 mm for the second part after the same cut; this arrangement does not fit. The 205 × 100 mm area is 20,500 mm² = 10,000 + 10,000 mm² of parts + 500 mm² of kerf loss.
- A 100 × 50 mm part exactly matching a 100 × 50 mm untrimmed piece requires **zero cuts** and has no kerf loss. To obtain that part from a 105 × 50 mm piece with a 5 mm kerf, **one edge-shaving cut** consumes the remaining 5 × 50 mm entirely as kerf, leaving no usable strip: 5,250 = 5,000 + 250 mm². From a 103 × 50 mm piece, only 3 mm remains: the full 5 mm kerf cannot fit inside the piece, so this isolation is **unsupported by this cutting model**, not physically impossible. A shop might use a blade extending over the outer edge, but this plan cannot represent that operation.
- Edge trims are **total loss measured from each original edge, including kerf**, not waste-strip width plus kerf. Zero means no trim cut; each positive allowance must be at least the kerf. For example, a 7 mm left trim with a 5 mm kerf discards a 2 mm strip plus the 5 mm blade band. Trims run in fixed **left, right, bottom, top** order, each across the piece retained by previous trims. On a 100 × 100 mm piece, 5 mm on each edge takes **four cuts** and leaves a 90 × 90 mm rectangle starting at (5, 5) mm. The first two passes lose 5 × 100 = 500 mm² each; the next two lose 5 × 90 = 450 mm² each. Thus 10,000 = 8,100 mm² usable + **1,900 mm² trim loss**, with corners counted once. If that usable rectangle is the finished part, no further cuts are needed.

For area accounting, original stock area = finished-part area + recoverable rectangular offcut area + other discarded waste + non-trim kerf loss + trim loss. Trim loss already includes both its blade bands and discarded strips; do not add trim kerf again. These are planning assumptions, not shop approval or safe-handling certification. **Confirm the actual blade kerf, edge-trimming practice, grain/material/thickness, cut order and workholding with the shop before cutting.**

## Cost estimate and optimization

**Estimated new spending** adds the full per-piece price of each *used* **To purchase** sheet or offcut to the flat fee for every witnessed physical cut on *all* used stock. Trimming and edge-shaving passes count; an already existing stock boundary does not, and there is no assumed stacking discount. An **Owned** piece has no new material purchase expense, but its consumption and cuts still appear. Unused declared stock is not purchased by this estimate. For example, an owned offcut plus one used sheet priced at 200, with six physical cuts at 5 each, gives 200 material + 30 cutting = **230** in the project's currency. These are estimates, not shop quotations: **taxes, delivery, setup fees and stacking discounts are excluded**. Confirm the shop's rates and cutting practice. Changing the interface language never converts the project's currency or amounts.

Blank purchase prices and a blank cut fee mean **unknown**, not free. An entered `0` is an explicitly known zero. Known material and cutting subtotals remain visible when available, but a missing used-sheet price or required fee makes the total **incomplete**, never zero by default. Incomplete or unverified allocations likewise have no complete spending total. An unknown-cost candidate cannot support a lowest-spending claim, even if another candidate has known costs.

**Start optimization** searches the finite declared stock pool for complete, compatible allocations with independently verified full-span cut sequences; it respects grain, kerf and trims. Locked placements retain their sheet, coordinates and orientation. This is a **bounded heuristic search**, so its result is **best found within the search budget**, not a guaranteed global optimum. Failure to find a complete plan before the limit means none was found *within that budget*, not that cutting is impossible. A complete feasible current layout is retained as an incumbent; if no improvement is found it remains available and is not silently replaced. Unallocated or conflicted drafts cannot win a complete-plan ranking.

Choose an objective before searching:

- **Lowest estimated new spending:** full prices of used to-purchase stock plus the fee per physical cut on owned and purchased stock. Ties prefer less unused used-stock area, then fewer cuts. Unknown prices/fees are sorted after known totals but cannot establish a cheapest-plan claim.
- **Fewest physical cuts:** count actual witnessed saw passes, including trims, once per split rather than per part. Ties prefer less unused used-stock area, then comparable complete new spending.
- **Least unused stock area:** sum the **full area of every used stock piece minus finished-part area**. This includes reusable offcuts as well as irreversible losses; unused stock pieces are excluded. Ties prefer fewer cuts, then comparable complete new spending. For example, 10,000 mm² of stock yielding 8,000 mm² of parts and 1,000 mm² each of offcuts and loss has 2,000 mm² unused; 12,000 mm² yielding the same parts, 3,500 mm² offcuts and 500 mm² loss has 4,000 mm² unused. The first wins this objective despite greater irreversible loss. The preview reports reusable offcuts separately from irreversible waste, blade loss and trim loss; utilization uses the full area of used stock as its denominator.

Remaining ties use stable stock/part identities and placements for reproducible ordering. The comparative preview shows the current layout against the best found, including stock pieces used, cuts, offcuts, unused area, irreversible loss, spending completeness and changed placements. **Accept best found** is required to replace placements as one undoable edit; merely searching does not change the project. If the current plan already matches, acceptance is a no-op. **Cancel search** leaves placements and undo history untouched. Changes to relevant manufacturing inputs make a result stale: start a new search before accepting. Acceptance revalidates the complete cutting witnesses and locks; a stale or invalid result cannot be applied.
