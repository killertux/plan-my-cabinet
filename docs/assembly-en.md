# Assemblies and hierarchy

Open **Design** from the five-entry rail. Its left **Outliner** shows the object
hierarchy; the right inspector changes with the selected board, assembly or
multiple objects. At a compact window, reopen collapsed controls or the
inspector from the header without losing a pending edit. The outliner has
separate expansion and visibility controls: collapsing a group does not hide
its geometry, and hiding it does not erase a stock warning. Advanced hierarchy
actions remain below the object list, including grouping, reparenting,
ungrouping and duplication. The scene and outliner share selection, but
selecting a stock sheet does not select all its boards.

The **Outliner** shows assemblies and boards indented under their parent. Click a row to select it; Shift-click or Command-click to add or remove a row. Selecting an assembly highlights its descendant boards in the viewport. **Frame selection** includes its visible boards.

**Group selection** creates an assembly at the displayed world-space pivot and moves the selected roots into it without moving their geometry. **Reparent selection** moves selected roots to another assembly or the world root; **Ungroup assembly** removes the active assembly and promotes its children. Cycles are rejected. Each accepted operation is one undoable edit; Cancel and invalid inputs leave the project untouched.

**Transform selection** translates in world-space millimetres and rotates in XYZ degrees about the displayed world-space pivot. A selected assembly and its selected descendant move together only once. Position inputs use 0.001 mm precision; nonrepresentable entries require explicit rounding confirmation. An assembly is a rigid hierarchy: moving its parent moves its descendants, but editing one board's length, width or thickness does not resize its siblings. Face placement and drag snaps store a pose only; they do not parent boards or create a joint. A mechanical relationship such as a hinge is a separate, explicit hardware/joint operation. Grouping, duplicating or placing boards never implicitly creates a hinge or door motion.

**Duplicate assembly** copies the active assembly, all nested assemblies, boards and attached reference hardware. Enter an optional world-space X/Y/Z offset (zero keeps the same position). The new root becomes selected. Every copied object has a new identity and keeps its relative pose and material/catalog references. Copied boards are separate physical parts: their originals' sheet allocations are **not** copied. Instead, each new board independently attempts first fit against the *current* stock and kerf in the same undoable transaction; it remains unallocated if no verified fit exists. Existing hinge installations and door relationships keep references to the original objects and are not automatically copied or rewired. Inspect the copied hardware and any required relationships explicitly before manufacturing.

Use **Hide** beside a group or board to remove it from the 3D scene; descendants of hidden groups disappear too. The object list marks hidden rows and remains usable to select them. **Reveal** on a hidden row also reveals any hidden ancestors. Framing ignores hidden geometry; a selection without visible frameable geometry disables **Frame selection**. Clear the selection to frame the visible scene. Hide/reveal are session-only display choices, not document edits: they do not affect undo, save revision, board demand or allocations. Undoing a deletion retains hide choices for surviving objects and drops choices for removed objects.

Choose **Measure** above the viewport for a read-only bounding-dimensions readout, also shown in the inspector. It measures the selected object rows, including descendants of selected assemblies without counting a selected descendant twice. Select multiple rows with Shift/Command; hardware has its own labelled row in the hierarchy and can be selected independently. Choose **Body only · wooden boards** to exclude all hardware or **Overall · boards + selected hardware** to include explicitly selected hardware and hardware under selected assemblies. Both are bounding boxes (X × Y × Z), not sums of nominal heights: feet entirely inside the body's extents add nothing. Hidden selected descendants still contribute. Choose **World** or any assembly/board as the **Frame**; dimensions follow that frame's axes even when nested groups are rotated. Scope and frame remain selected when leaving and returning to Measure. The readout identifies scope, frame and display units; values are displayed to three decimal places, but calculations use unrounded spatial coordinates and do not edit manufacturing lengths. Hardware without solid dimensions makes Overall bounds unavailable, with an explanation instead of an invented size. Board-local length/width/thickness remain separate editable dimensions.

In a board's **Numeric pose** dialog, **Lay flat** absolutely aligns its local length X, width Y and thickness Z with the selected **World** or **Local parent** frame axes (broad X/Y face in the frame X/Y plane). **Stand up** absolutely maps local X to frame +Z, local Y to +Y and local Z to −X (−90° about frame Y). **Turn 90° Z** rotates the current tentative pose *relatively* +90° about the selected frame's +Z. Each uses the board's **pose origin**, not its centre, as pivot; inspect the disclosed frame, axis/face result, pivot, numeric fields and derived extents. Presets update only the cancellable preview. **Cancel** or Escape restores the original pose and selection without a project edit; **Apply** accepts a valid proposal as one undoable placement. The dialog's World/Local-parent coordinates and manual rotation fields remain available.

Scene shortcuts work only when the viewport has keyboard focus and no dialog is open. Delete currently has no scene deletion command; pressing Delete while typing edits text only. Modal dialogs block hierarchy selection, transforms, viewport picking and dragging until dismissed.

## Build an example body from individual boards

This is a reproducible placement example, not a cabinet template or a construction recommendation. Dimensions below are millimetres; the body spans X = 0–820 (width), Y = 0–600 (depth), Z = 0–2300 (height). Board-local X/Y/Z mean length/width/thickness. No cabinet preset or hinge is involved.

1. In Design's left controls choose **New material**. Name it “18 mm panel”, enter thickness **18 mm** and choose the desired grain. Choose **New board**, select that material, and create “Left side” with length **2300** and width **600**. Create “Bottom” with length **784** and width **600** and “Shelf” with the same dimensions. Each starts at the origin and has its own physical ID.
2. On the Left side's board row choose **Numeric pose**. Choose **World**, enter position **X 18, Y 0, Z 0 mm** and rotation **X 0°, Y −90°, Z 0°**; confirm. Its thickness now extends from X 0 to 18 and its length rises in Z. On Bottom choose **Numeric pose → World** and enter **X 18, Y 0, Z 0 mm**, leaving rotation at zero; confirm.
3. On Left side choose **Duplicate board**. The copy starts 25 mm along its *local parent* X; it is a distinct part. On the copy's row choose **Numeric pose → World**, set **X 820, Y 0, Z 0 mm** and keep its rotation **Y −90°**; confirm. Its thickness spans X 802–820. Duplicating a board does not copy any stock allocation; edit the copy independently.
4. On Shelf choose **Place face to face**. Choose Bottom as target, source face **Thickness Z -**, target face **Thickness Z +**, **Keep start face → Keep start face** on both in-plane axes, both offsets **0 mm**, and outward gap **1082 mm**. Inspect the cyan/pink face preview and confirm: Shelf starts at Z 1100. To add another shelf, choose **Duplicate board** on Shelf, then **Numeric pose → World** on the copy and set **X 18, Y 0, Z 1500 mm** (rotation zero). To make the top, duplicate Bottom and set its copy's world position to **X 18, Y 0, Z 2282 mm**. Confirm each pose. The gap here is a one-time position, not a persistent clearance rule.
5. In **Outliner**, click one board and Shift-click the others, then **Group selection**. Name the group “Body”, keep its world pivot at **0, 0, 0 mm** and its parent at **World root**, and confirm. The boards keep their world poses. Select Body to move all descendants once with **Transform selection**; choose **Reparent selection** to put selected roots under another assembly (or World root), and **Ungroup assembly** to promote children without moving them. These operations do not couple board sizes. **Duplicate assembly** makes another independent subtree with fresh IDs and an optional world X/Y/Z offset.
6. Select Body, choose **Measure → Body only · wooden boards → Frame: World** and inspect the readout: X × Y × Z is **820 × 600 × 2300 mm**. Select an assembly or board in **Frame** to read extents along that object's axes instead. **Overall · boards + selected hardware** includes only selected hardware or hardware descending from selected assemblies; it reports the actual bounding box, not body height plus a nominal foot height. Hiding a board does not exclude it from the measurement when its assembly is selected.

For less exact rough placement, select **Move board** above the viewport and drag from a visible board surface. A visible face candidate takes precedence over an XY grid intersection; the status identifies which one is previewed. Hold **Alt** while dragging to bypass both snaps, release for one pose edit, or press **Escape** to cancel. Set grid spacing with **Edit grid spacing** in Settings → Grid & units or the Design snap menu; changing it does not move existing boards. Use **Numeric pose** in **Local parent** for coordinates relative to a group, or **World** for absolute placement. Position fields accept unit suffixes and require confirmation when rounded to 0.001 mm; rotations are XYZ degrees. **Cancel** leaves the original pose intact.
